# Cold Email Subject Lines That Get Opened (Without Clickbait)

*Your subject line doesn't sell your product. It sells the decision to open. Here's what actually works — and what gets you spam-foldered.*

Most founders write subject lines like they're writing an ad headline: clever, punchy, mysterious. Then they wonder why open rates sit at 20%. The truth is boring: **the subject line's job is just to make the recipient think "this is for me, and it's short."** Everything else happens in the body.

---

## What the data keeps showing

Across the cold email benchmarks we've tracked (and the patterns behind tools like ours):

- **Personalized > clever.** Subject lines with the prospect's company name or a specific reference consistently outperform generic "creativity."
- **Short wins.** 4-8 words outperforms longer lines. The recipient scans it in under a second while half-working.
- **Relevant beats intriguing.** "Question about your [feature/product]" beats "A quick thought" every time.
- **No gimmicks.** ALL CAPS, excessive punctuation, "RE:", and "URGENT" get you spam-foldered before anyone reads a word.

## Subject line templates that work

**The reference line** (works when you know something specific):

> Question about your [X]
> Quick thought on [their recent launch/post]
> [Their company]'s [specific feature]

**The outcome line** (works when your value is concrete):

> Cutting reply times for [their use case]
> [Metric] improvement for [their workflow]
> A faster way to [their job-to-be-done]

**The curiosity line** (use sparingly — only when the body genuinely delivers):

> Not a pitch, just a question
> Wanted your take on this

## What your body needs to justify the line

A subject line that gets an open gets a *bounce* if the body doesn't deliver. Three rules:

1. **Personalize in the first sentence.** "I saw [your team] is hiring a Growth Lead and launched [feature] last month — congrats on both." If you can't write this sentence, you don't know the prospect well enough to email them.
2. **One reason to reply.** End with a single, easy question. Not three. Not a demo request. One question they can answer in 30 seconds.
3. **Short.** 90-120 words. Nobody reads a 600-word cold email.

## The deliverability side you can't skip

A great subject line means nothing if the email never lands:

- SPF, DKIM, DMARC configured before your first send
- Domain warmed up (10-20/day ramping over 2-3 weeks)
- Verified addresses — every bounce poisons your reputation
- Plain-text format, no image-heavy HTML

---

## The 60-second subject line checklist

- [ ] Under 8 words
- [ ] Contains something specific to them (name, company, feature)
- [ ] No ALL CAPS, no "RE:", no exclamation marks
- [ ] Honest — the body delivers exactly what it promises
- [ ] Passes the spam filter test (not just looks, but *reads* like a person wrote it)
