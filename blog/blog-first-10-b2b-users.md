# How We Got Our First 10 B2B SaaS Users (No Ads, No Budget)

*The honest, unglamorous playbook we're running right now — and what actually moves the needle.*

If you're a B2B SaaS founder starting from zero — no audience, no network, no ad budget — the standard advice ("send 100 cold emails a day, post on X, submit to 50 directories") feels like a lottery ticket. After months of running this exact gauntlet, here's what we've learned about getting first users with $0.

## 1. Cold email is still the highest-leverage channel — IF you personalize

Mass blasts get zero replies. Full stop. Twenty genuinely tailored emails to the right ICP will outperform 500 generic ones every time.

What "personalized" actually means in practice:

- Reference something specific about their company — a recent feature launch, a pricing page detail, a blog post they wrote.
- Show you understand their buyer. Not "Hi {First Name}" — that's a template variable, not personalization.
- Be short. 90-120 words. One question. One clear reason to reply.

## 2. Don't burn your sending domain

Most first-time founders kill their cold email before it starts:

- Sending 100+ emails/day from a brand-new domain
- Zero warm-up
- No SPF/DKIM/DMARC setup
- Buying scraped lists full of dead addresses

Bounce rate above 2% and spam complaints will tank your domain reputation in weeks. Verify addresses, warm up gradually, and check your authentication records before sending anything.

## 3. X (Twitter) is a trust layer, not a distribution channel

Building in public is worth it — at ~400 followers we started getting inbound DMs from founders who saw our threads. But it is *slow*. Expect weeks of daily posting before it compounds. Treat it as credibility you build *while* you run faster channels, not as your primary acquisition path.

## 4. Directories are a slow drip, not a spike

Yes, submit to the free directories (Product Hunt, BetaList, and the long tail of AI tool lists). Each one brings a few visits. Combined they add up over weeks — but none of them will be your first customer moment. Submit once, then forget about them and move on.

## 5. Comment where your ICP actually hangs out

r/SaaS, r/marketing, and niche Slack communities won't drive traffic if you drop links. But genuinely useful comments — answering a founder's question with real specifics — get you DMs, profile visits, and trust. We've had founders reach out directly after a helpful comment. One useful comment beats ten self-promotional posts.

## 6. Expect 3-6 weeks for the first 10

The honest timeline: if you send 20-30 tailored emails a day, comment usefully in 2-3 communities, and keep a consistent X presence, the first paying customers usually take 3-6 weeks. Most founders quit at week 2. The compounding doesn't feel like anything until it suddenly does.

### The checklist

- ICP defined down to 50 specific companies
- Cold email: 20-30 tailored/day, verified addresses, bounce <2%
- Domain warmed up, SPF/DKIM/DMARC in place
- X: one valuable post/day, genuine engagement
- Reddit/communities: 1-2 useful comments/day
- Directories: submitted once, then ignored
- Measure weekly, not daily

*No ads. No budget. Just consistent, tailored, deliverability-first outreach — plus one honest question: who is your email actually written for?*
